# Finishing — the production sand-and-paint process

FDM prints show layer lines, and a bumper part lives in direct sun. Production
kits were finished with the process below, which is where their smooth,
factory-like look comes from. It applies to the **housings** (the visible parts);
retainers live behind the bumper and can stay raw.

If you printed in ASA and like the printed texture, you can skip paint — ASA is
UV-stable on its own. Painting is what makes the parts look injection-molded, and
it matters even more if you printed in a less UV-stable material.

## Supplies (what production used)

- Sandpaper: ~220 grit and 400 grit (use the 400 wet)
- **[SEM 42003 Black High Build Primer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000PL07B6)** —
  automotive high-build/filler primer, 16 oz aerosol
- **[SEM 49143 Trim Black Ultra](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07NDM9SPB)** — satin
  black trim paint, UV-resistant, formulated for plastics

## Process

1. **Sand the visible surfaces** with ~220 grit to knock down the layer lines.
   Stay off the mounting flange's mating face and out of the light cutout and
   bolt holes — dimensions matter there.
2. **First primer coats:** 2–3 coats of high-build primer, following the can's
   recoat times. The primer's job is to fill the remaining layer texture.
3. **Wet-sand with 400 grit** until smooth. If lines still telegraph through,
   prime and sand again — the finish you see at the end is the finish you create
   at this step.
4. **Top coat:** 2–3 light coats of UV-resistant satin black. Light coats beat
   one heavy coat; heavy coats run on the housing's curves.
5. **Cure fully before assembly** — solvent-soft paint will fingerprint when you
   press the housing into the bumper.

## Keep paint out of

- The **heat-set insert bosses** on the retainers (if you paint them at all)
- The **bracket cutout and center hole** — the light's articulation is a
  clearance fit; paint buildup tightens it
- The housing's **rear wire hole**
